Аnimation

Images viewed at a rate of 16 or more frames per second, give the illusion of continuous motion.

What is animation?

Animation is a technique in which each frame of a film is produced individually, either through generation as in computer graphics, or by photographing cartoon images, or by continuously making small changes to a model and photographing the result. When the frames are connected and the resulting film is viewed at a rate of 16 or more frames per second, the illusion of continuous motion is created. Creating such a film is a laborious process, although the development of computer animation has considerably sped up the process.
Graphic formats such as GIF, MNG, and Flash allow for animations on a computer or the internet.

Computer animation

Computer animation is a field of computer graphics and animation. It is most often created in 3D graphics, less often in 2D graphics. Sometimes it is performed with computer-generated images, especially when used in films, although computer-generated images can be used in the production of computer animation but are not animation in themselves. The technique of creating the illusion that an image is moving is identical to the technique used in the production of films, i.e. television shows. Namely, in order to create the illusion of a moving image, it is necessary on the monitor to replace the current image with the next image that is slightly modified at high speed, so that the viewer has the illusion that the image is moving. 2D computer animation is used in the production of cartoons. Unlike in the past when the images that simulate movement were drawn by hand, today computer-generated drawings are used in drawn films. In this way, films are produced much faster by hiring a smaller number of animators and the cost is insignificant. Recently, films made with 3D animation have been very popular.
